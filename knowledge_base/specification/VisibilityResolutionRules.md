# VisibilityResolutionRules.md

## Purpose

The purpose of this specification is to define how visibility is resolved during simulation execution.

The Visibility Ontology defines:

- who can observe what
- visibility levels
- observation restrictions

However, the ontology intentionally does not define the operational semantics of observation generation.

This specification fills that gap.

The objective is to transform visibility declarations into deterministic execution rules.

The specification does not define:

- probabilities
- likelihoods
- statistical assumptions
- predictive models
- behavioural realism

The simulation environment intentionally avoids hidden probabilistic assumptions.

Therefore visibility resolution must remain deterministic and auditable.

Visibility evaluation determines whether an observation is generated from a simulation artefact.

---

# Core Principles

## Deterministic Visibility Principle

Visibility resolution must be deterministic.

Given:

- the same world state
- the same visibility rules
- the same events
- the same situations
- the same interventions

the same observations must be generated.

Visibility resolution must never require random sampling.

---

## Explicit Information Principle

Information only becomes visible when explicitly permitted.

No agent may gain information through:

- implicit inference
- hidden assumptions
- implementation shortcuts
- undefined reasoning

Information access must always be explained through a visibility rule.

---

## Information Asymmetry Principle

Different agents may possess different information.

The existence of an event does not imply universal visibility.

The existence of an intervention does not imply universal visibility.

The existence of a situation does not imply universal visibility.

Information asymmetry is an intentional property of the simulation environment.

---

## Ontology Compliance Principle

Visibility resolution cannot create new artefacts.

The visibility process only evaluates existing:

- Events
- Situation States
- Observations
- Interventions

Visibility resolution does not generate:

- new events
- new situations
- new interventions

Visibility resolution only determines what becomes observable.

---

# Visibility Evaluation Targets

Visibility evaluation applies to four categories of simulation artefacts.

---

## Events

Events are first-class visibility targets.

Examples:

- Friend request sent
- Personal information shared
- Child report submitted
- Parent notified
- Contact limitation applied

An event may be visible to:

- one actor
- multiple actors
- all actors

depending on the Visibility Ontology.

---

## Situation States

Situation states may become observable.

Examples:

- Trust building
- Dependency formation
- Emotional pressure
- Ambiguous signals
- Coordinated protection effort

Observation of a situation does not require observation of every underlying event.

A protection actor may observe that a situation exists without observing the complete interaction history.

---

## Observations

Observations are themselves visibility targets.

Examples:

- Teacher observation
- Parent observation
- Platform observation
- Protection-system observation

An agent does not automatically gain access to observations generated by another observer.

Access depends on visibility rules and information-sharing events.

---

## Interventions

Interventions may be visible to different actors.

Examples:

- Educational content
- Parent notification
- Teacher support
- Temporary restriction
- Contact limitation

The intervention itself may be visible even when the rationale remains hidden.

---

# Visibility Categories

Every visibility declaration must belong to one of the following categories.

---

## Can Observe

Meaning:

Observation generation is mandatory whenever the matching artefact exists.

No additional condition is required.

No probability is involved.

---

### Resolution Rule

If:

- artefact exists
- visibility declaration is "Can observe"

then:

- observation must be generated

---

### Example

Child

Can observe:

- Friend request accepted

If a Friend request accepted event exists,

the Child observation is always generated.

---

## Can Sometimes Observe

Meaning:

Observation generation requires an enabling visibility condition.

Observation is not automatic.

Observation is not probabilistic.

The visibility condition must be explicitly represented in the simulation.

---

### Resolution Rule

If:

- artefact exists
- visibility declaration is "Can sometimes observe"
- enabling condition exists

then:

- observation is generated

Otherwise:

- observation is not generated

---

### Example

Parent Unit

Can sometimes observe:

- Increased online activity

The observation is only generated when an enabling condition exists.

Examples of enabling conditions:

- device monitoring enabled
- social-media supervision enabled
- child disclosure occurred
- platform notification occurred

Without an enabling condition no observation is created.

---

### Rationale

This approach preserves:

- determinism
- reproducibility
- transparency

while still permitting policy-dependent information access.

---

## Cannot Observe

Meaning:

Observation generation is forbidden.

No condition may override this restriction.

---

### Resolution Rule

If:

- visibility declaration is "Cannot observe"

then:

- no observation may be generated

---

### Example

Parent Unit

Cannot observe:

- private platform conversations

The simulator must never create such an observation unless another event explicitly changes visibility through escalation or disclosure.

---

# Enabling Visibility Conditions

## Purpose

Enabling conditions determine when conditional visibility becomes active.

The Visibility Ontology identifies who may conditionally observe something.

This specification defines how that decision is operationalized.

---

## Condition Sources

An enabling visibility condition may originate from:

- scenario configuration
- policy configuration
- active intervention
- active restriction
- active relationship
- active situation
- explicit information-sharing event

No other condition sources are permitted.

---

## Scenario-Derived Conditions

A scenario may activate visibility conditions at initialization.

Examples:

- parent monitoring exists
- teacher regularly observes behaviour
- platform moderation enabled

These conditions remain active until changed by execution.

---

## Policy-Derived Conditions

A policy may explicitly enable information access.

Examples:

- proactive parent alerts
- teacher notification policy
- platform transparency policy

---

## Situation-Derived Conditions

Certain situations may activate additional visibility.

Examples:

Situation:

- Ambiguous signals

May enable:

- ProtectionSystem observations

Situation:

- Coordinated protection effort

May enable:

- parent visibility
- teacher visibility
- psychologist visibility

---

## Intervention-Derived Conditions

Interventions may modify visibility.

Examples:

Parent notification

creates a visibility pathway toward ParentUnit.

Teacher support

creates a visibility pathway toward Teacher.

Psychologist informed

creates a visibility pathway toward Psychologist.

---

## Event-Derived Conditions

Events may directly alter visibility.

Examples:

- Child report submitted
- Parent notified
- Teacher informed
- Psychologist informed
- External referral initiated

Such events create explicit information flow.

---

# Visibility Resolution Algorithm

## Step 1

Select artefact.

Possible artefacts:

- Event
- SituationState
- Observation
- Intervention

---

## Step 2

Identify candidate observers.

Candidate observers are determined by the Visibility Ontology.

---

## Step 3

Evaluate visibility category.

Possible categories:

- Can observe
- Can sometimes observe
- Cannot observe

---

## Step 4

Apply resolution rule.

Can observe

→ Observation generated.

Can sometimes observe

→ Evaluate enabling conditions.

Cannot observe

→ Observation forbidden.

---

## Step 5

Generate observation records.

Successful visibility evaluations create observations.

Observation generation must follow the Observation Ontology.

---

# Visibility Escalation

## Purpose

Visibility may expand over time.

Information that was previously private may become restricted or public.

---

## Escalation Sources

Visibility escalation may occur through:

- reporting
- support requests
- interventions
- referrals
- coordinated protection activities

---

## Example

Initial state:

Conversation

Visibility:

local

Visible to:

- Child
- ThreatActor

Event:

Child report submitted

Result:

Conversation details may become visible to:

- ProtectionSystem

Event:

Parent notified

Result:

Relevant case information becomes visible to:

- ParentUnit

The original event remains unchanged.

Only visibility changes.

---

# Visibility Levels

Visibility levels define the scope of information access.

---

## public

Visible to every participating actor.

Example:

Contact blocked

---

## local

Visible only to directly involved actors.

Example:

Conversation

---

## restricted

Visible only after an enabling event or escalation.

Example:

Teacher informed

Parent notified

Psychologist informed

---

## private

Visible only to the originating actor.

Examples:

- internal moderation result
- platform detection output
- protection-system reasoning

---

# Information Flow Constraints

## No Automatic Propagation

Information never propagates automatically.

An observation created for one actor does not automatically become visible to another actor.

Additional visibility rules are required.

---

## No Omniscient Observer

No actor is assumed to possess complete knowledge of the environment.

Even the ProtectionSystem may only access information available through configured channels.

---

## No Reverse Visibility

Visibility is not symmetric.

Example:

Parent may observe:

- Parent notification

This does not imply that the Child observes:

- Parent reasoning

---

# Consistency Requirements

## Determinism Requirement

Visibility resolution must not depend on randomness.

---

## Explainability Requirement

Every generated observation must be explainable through:

- a visibility rule
- an enabling condition

---

## Auditability Requirement

Every visibility decision must be reproducible from the simulation trace.

---

## Completeness Requirement

Every eligible visibility rule must be evaluated during execution.

Visibility decisions may not be skipped.

---

## Ontology Compliance Requirement

Visibility rules may only reference artefacts defined by:

- EventOntology
- SituationOntology
- ObservationOntology
- InterventionOntology

No undefined artefacts may participate in visibility evaluation.
